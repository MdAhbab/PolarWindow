# ADR-11: The trip engine is ours, checked against GMAT and flown missions

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-09-26
**Relates to:** ADR-1 (the expensive step is precomputed), ADR-12 (jobs)

## Context

The trip (launch to lunar orbit) needs a force model with the Sun, Earth and
Moon, Earth's oblateness, the Moon's lumpy gravity, sunlight pressure and,
for weeks-long Earth-orbit loops, air drag. Options: orekit (Java, via JPype),
heyoka (LLVM Taylor integrator) or our own on numpy, scipy and numba.

## Decision

1. **Our own**, in pure `core/` modules (`forces.py`, `propagate.py`,
   `lambert.py`, `transfer.py`, `launchwin.py`), because it adds no
   dependency, reads the same SPICE ephemeris and frames as the surface
   engine (one source of truth for time and frames), and keeps the fast test
   suite fast.
2. **Credibility is earned by tests, not by a brand**: NASA GMAT R2026a is the
   offline reference (scripts and outputs committed, regenerated by
   `make gmat-golden`), and the reconstructed trajectories of IM-1, IM-2,
   Blue Ghost 1 and Peregrine from NAIF's CLPS SPICE archive are the ground
   truth. Tolerances are in `docs/validation.md` and the plan.
3. **Frames and time are never defaulted**: J2000 and TDB for integration,
   MOON_PA for lunar gravity (GRGM1200A is defined there), MOON_ME for the
   surface, ITRF93 for pads and stations. Every SPICE call names its frame,
   observer and aberration correction (`core/spice.py`).
4. GMs come from `gm_de440.tpc`, in TDB units. Mixing the IERS TT value would
   drift by metres a day.

## Consequences

- We carry the maintenance of an integrator; the GMAT and flown-mission tests
  are the guard.
- orekit can be added later as an optional cross-check without being imported
  by the app.
