### Prompt 1: low Sun over the pole

```
A slow, wide, cinematic shot of the south pole of the Moon, filmed as if by a real camera on a small
spacecraft flying low and level above the ground. The ground is grey, dry and very rough: old craters of
many sizes, sharp rims, scattered boulders and fine dust, with no air, no haze, no clouds and no wind. The
sky is pure black. There are no stars visible, because the bright ground hides them, as in real photographs
taken on the Moon.

The Sun is a small, very bright white disc sitting just above the horizon at the far edge of the picture.
It does not rise. It stays at the same low height for the whole shot. Because the Sun is so low, every
crater rim and every rock throws a very long, hard-edged, completely black shadow across the ground. The
lit ground is bright and slightly warm in colour. The shadows are deep black with no detail inside them.
About half of the ground in view is in shadow. Some crater floors are fully dark.

The camera moves forward slowly and smoothly at a steady speed, with a very small turn to the left, so the
long shadows slide gently across the picture. There is no shaking, no zoom and no cut. The movement is
calm and slow, like a documentary.

The look is realistic and photographic: natural contrast, fine detail in the dust and rocks, a slight film
grain, no glow effects and no lens flare rings around the Sun. Colours are natural: grey ground, black sky,
white Sun with a slightly warm tint on the lit ground.

Do not show any spacecraft, lander, rover, astronaut, person, flag, building, text, logo, numbers or
symbols. Do not show Earth. Do not show a blue sky, an atmosphere, clouds, water, plants, or more than one
Sun. Do not make the picture look like a cartoon, a painting or a video game.

Length 8 seconds, 16:9 wide screen, 24 frames per second, highest quality, no sound.
```

The camera does not move at all. There is no zoom, no shake and no cut. The look is photographic and
natural: hard light, deep black shadows, fine detail in the dust and foil, slight film grain, no glow and

## 4. Set up the edit

Use the editor you know best (DaVinci Resolve, Premiere or CapCut all work).

- **Project:** 1920 x 1080, 30 frames per second.
- **Fonts** (free, from Google Fonts): **Archivo** for all titles and labels. **IBM Plex Mono** for the
  small captions. No other fonts.
- **Colours:** background `#07090B`. Words `#E6EBF0`. Sun amber `#F2A33C`. Earth cyan `#4FC6E0`.
  Panel grey `#11161B`. Use amber only for things about the Sun and cyan only for things about Earth.
- **No effects:** every change of picture is a plain cut, or a 6-frame fade. No spins, no flashes, no
  sound effects.
- **Folders in the project:** `faces`, `screen`, `film`, `figures`, `sound`, `text`.

### Build these five pieces first

1. **The split frame** (used for every face shot). The face clip is cropped to its half and fills one
   half of the screen (960 x 1080). The other half is the background colour with the figure centred,
   60 pixels of space around it. Make one version with the face on the left and one with the face on the
   right.
2. **The name caption.** First name and role, for example "Wali · Research". Archivo semi-bold, 36 px,
   on a dark rounded box at the bottom of the person's half. Shown for the first 4 seconds each person
   appears in their own part.
3. **The title card** (2 seconds). Background colour, the PolarWindow logo, the team name large, and under
   it: "Challenge: CLPS Lunar Mission Browser".
4. **The name strip** (10 seconds). The screen is split into six tall strips, each 320 pixels wide. Each
   strip shows one person's "I'm ..." clip, cropped to their face, with their name and role at the bottom.
   The strips appear one at a time, left to right, every 1.6 seconds: Wali, Ryan, Ahbab, Mehrab, Tanjamul,
   Mehedi.
   Each strip stays on screen once it has appeared. This way every name is readable for a long time.
5. **The end card** (6 seconds). The film clip, darkened, with: the logo, "PolarWindow",
   `clpsspaceapps.ahbab.dev`, `github.com/MdAhbab/PolarWindow`, the team name, and all six names in one
   line.

## 6. Sound

1. Line up each face clip with its separate sound file, using the clap. Use the separate sound if it is
   cleaner than the phone's.
2. On every voice clip: a light noise cleaner, then set the loudness so all six voices sound equally
   loud. The loudest moments should reach about -3 dB and never touch 0.
3. Cut out breaths, clicks and "um" between sentences. Leave about a third of a second between sentences.
4. **Music:** one calm track with no singing, from the YouTube Audio Library (free to use). Write down its
   name and artist: it goes in the video description. Under a voice the music is very quiet (about -24 dB).
   On the title card and the end card, where nobody speaks, raise it (about -12 dB). Fade it out over the
   last 2 seconds.
5. Mute the sound that comes with the film clips and the screen recordings.
6. Listen to the whole video once on phone speakers and once on earphones. Every word must be clear on both.

## 10. If something goes wrong

| Problem | What to do |
|---|---|
| A face clip is noisy or dark | Ask that person to record again at once. It takes five minutes. Do not try to fix it for an hour. |
| A person's clip is missing at 11:00 AM | Use their voice only, over the figure for their part at full screen. |
| The video is too long | Shorten the name strip to 8 seconds and the end card to 4. Then trim silences. |
| A generated figure has broken text or odd shapes | Make it again. After three tries, use the real app screen, or ask Ahbab for an exact diagram. |
| A screen recording shows something loading | Cut the wait out. If it still looks wrong, ask Ahbab for that one shot again. |
| A team member is under 18 | Their face must not be recognisable. Use their voice over their figure, with their name on screen. |
